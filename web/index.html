    Voltage history

    Where these numbers come from

    • The chassis board reports only the pack voltage, in 0.1 V steps: no current, no percentage. The percentage comes from the open-circuit voltage curve of a 3-cell (3S) NMC lithium-ion pack, with 0% at 9.6 V, where Yahboom's board starts beeping. Whether the pack really is NMC (label "INR") or LiPo ("ICR" or a flat pouch) is not confirmed.
    • Voltage drops under load, so only readings taken at rest count: 5 s after the last motion command, smoothed over 20 s. While driving, the displayed value is held. It never goes up unless the voltage stays higher for 30 s or more (the pack was charged or swapped).
    • The drain rate is the slope of the last 15 minutes of resting readings, and needs at least 5 minutes of data. More sensors on means faster drain.
    • With Base off, the hub reads the voltage straight from the chassis board (battery monitor) and hands the serial port back before Base starts.
    • Warning at 10.5 V, finish at 10.0 V, alarm at 9.6 V; at 9.0 V the hub engages E-STOP. Store the pack at 11.1–11.7 V.

      Saved networks

        When several saved networks are in range, the car joins the one with the highest priority. Its own hotspot has priority 50 and always counts as "in range", so with no higher-priority network around the car opens the hotspot by itself. Networks added or re-keyed here get priority 60.

        Connecting in a new place

        • The car and your laptop can use the same Wi-Fi: pick that network here and enter its password. When the car has switched, move your laptop to the same network; this page then jumps to http://rosmaster.local:8080 by itself.
        • No known network around: after power-on the car opens its hotspot ROSMASTER (password 12345678). Join it, open http://192.168.1.11:8080 or http://rosmaster.local:8080, and set up Wi-Fi on this page.
        • rosmaster.local does not open: some campus and office networks block device discovery (mDNS) or even traffic between devices. Use the IP address on the car's small OLED screen: http://that-IP:8080. If the IP does not work either, the network isolates devices: use the car's hotspot.
        • Open networks and WPA/WPA2-Personal only. Networks that need a username (e.g. UCR-SECURE) are not supported.
